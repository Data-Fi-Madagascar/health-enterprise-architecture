## The challenge

Madagascar's digital health landscape is expanding rapidly but remains fragmented: parallel systems, duplicated data collection, limited interoperability, and investments whose link to health outcomes is hard to demonstrate. Without a common architectural reference, every new initiative risks adding to the fragmentation it intends to solve.

## The HEA response

The Health Enterprise Architecture (HEA) is Madagascar's single architectural reference for digital health — a structured, versioned body of knowledge that defines **where the sector is going, why, and how each system contributes**. It serves simultaneously as a strategic compass for decision-makers and a technical rulebook for implementers.

## Methodology

Two deliberate methodological choices:

1. **Value-driven design.** The architecture starts from expected results for beneficiaries, formalized as national value streams, then derives the capabilities required to deliver them — and only from there, principles, standards, decisions, and technical patterns. Every technical artifact traces back to a strategic outcome.
2. **Architecture as code.** All content is written in structured Markdown with machine-readable metadata, version-controlled, and continuously validated by automated scripts (3,000+ cross-references checked; published documents generated from a single source of truth). The result is a living, auditable asset — not a static report.

## A four-tier framework

| Tier | Framework | Purpose | Primary audience |
|------|-----------|---------|------------------|
| 1 | **CAESN** — Enterprise Architecture Framework | Strategy: value streams, capabilities, principles, data, governance | Decision-makers, programme owners |
| 2 | **CNISN** — National Interoperability Framework | Rules of exchange: standards, decision records, compliance | Architects, integrators |
| 3 | **ARTSN** — Reference Technical Architecture | Patterns, contracts, constraints, data dictionary, roadmap | Technical teams |
| 4 | **PTISN** — Implementation Profiles | Ready-to-use profiles per initiative, templates, target topology | Developers, vendors |

Each tier addresses its audience directly, with reading guides routing every stakeholder profile to the right entry point.

## Content highlights

- **Strategic layer** — 4 value streams, 18 enterprise capabilities with maturity assessment, data and application architectures, portfolio orientation
- **Interoperability layer** — 14 capabilities in 7 families, mandatory standards aligned with international norms, 10 documented architecture decisions, compliance programme
- **Technical layer** — 12+ architecture chapters, a shared dictionary of 31 sourced data objects across 7 domains, security and residence rules
- **Implementation layer** — 15 profiles covering priority initiatives, national target topology, interoperability test protocol and SLAs
- **Governance toolkit** — homologation process, decision registry and change management, architectural watch, deprecation policy, legal foundation supporting an e-health bill
- **Trajectory** — interoperability roadmap Q4 2026 – Q2 2030, phased technical roadmap aligned with national budgeting

## Why it matters

The HEA converts architecture from documentation into a **governance instrument**: new investments can be checked against a common reference before funding, existing systems positioned on a shared roadmap, and duplication avoided. It anchors Madagascar's digital health trajectory in measurable outcomes rather than isolated projects — and provides partners with a clear, stable framework for alignment.
